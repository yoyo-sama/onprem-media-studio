# MiniMax H3 video engines

French version: [fr/H3.md](fr/H3.md). Lessons behind each rule: [LESSONS.md](LESSONS.md) § 6.

MiniMax H3 renders video with synchronized stereo audio. 2.0 runs it through
[FastVideo](https://github.com/hao-ai-lab/FastVideo) (Apache-2.0), which publishes the distilled
FastH3 checkpoints with DGX Spark recipes. H3 replaces LTX 2.5 on every video path.

| Pipeline id | Input | Checkpoint (catalog id) | Steps | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `h3-t2v` | prompt | FastH3 V2 (`fasth3-v2-nvfp4`, fallback `fasth3-v2-fp8`) | 8 transformer forwards (9 sigma points), from the checkpoint | ≈0.9 MP, 1280×736 at 16:9 |
| `h3-i2v` | prompt + `image`, optional `last_image` (FL2VA) | same as `h3-t2v` | same | ≈0.9 MP at the first image's aspect |
| `h3-r2v` | prompt + 1 to 9 `references` | FastH3 OmniRef (`fasth3-omniref-nvfp4`, fallback `fasth3-omniref-fp8`) + latent upscaler (`h3-latent-upscaler`) | 8, then 3 refine steps | 736×416 → 1280×736 at 16:9 |

Every job writes `outputs/<pipeline>/<time>-<job>.mp4` (video and audio) and the clip's last
frame as a lossless PNG (`…-1.png`, with the job's parameters in a `studio` text chunk).

## Parameters

Common to the three engines:

| Parameter | Default | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `prompt` | required | Sent verbatim; at most 7,000 characters (refused above, never trimmed: trimming belongs to the front end's `capH3Prompt`, which cuts inside `detailed_description` only) |
| `duration` | 5 | Seconds, 5 to 15. Rendered frames follow the 17n+5 grid at 24 fps: 5 s → 124 frames (5.17 s) |
| `seed` | drawn | Recorded with the job |
| `loras` | `[]` | Up to 4 `{"name": "<file>.safetensors", "strength": -2…2}` from `STUDIO_DATA_DIR/loras/h3/` |
| `aspect` | `"16:9"` (i2v: the first image's) | `"W:H"`, 1:4 to 4:1 |
| `width`, `height` | from `aspect` | t2v and i2v only: multiples of 32, at most 768×1344 pixels |

`h3-i2v` adds `image` (first frame) and `last_image` (last frame, FL2VA). The prompt may cite
`<Picture 1>` (the first image) and, with a last image, `<Picture 2>`; anything else is refused.
FastVideo stretches the keyframes to the canvas, so the canvas follows the first image's aspect.

`h3-r2v` adds `references` (image references, in `<Picture N>` order: the first image is
`<Picture 1>`) and `preview` (`true`: first pass only, at 736×416). A prompt citing a
`<Picture N>` with no matching reference is refused. FastVideo resizes references to a
2048-pixel short edge, which is what `ref_image_size = max` did in the validated workflow.

No negative prompt: H3 runs without guidance, so `negative`/`negative_prompt` are refused.
Steps are not a parameter (LESSONS #2: under-stepping breaks colour first).

**Shorter clips are not possible.** 1.4 allowed cuts and Relay segments under 5 s; FastVideo's
MiniMax H3 pipelines accept 5 to 15 s only, so a shorter duration is refused with a message
saying so.

## Reference to video: the two passes

This is the ComfyUI workflow validated on the GB10 on 2026-10-28 (identity 0.838 at 1280×736,
5 s, 260 s), run through FastVideo without forking it:

1. **Pass 1**: FastH3 OmniRef, the checkpoint's 8-forward PDD plan (nodes 0, 4, …, 32 of the
   32-step fine grid), video shift 12 and audio shift 3 (from `fastvideo_inference.json`), VSA,
   at ≈0.3 MP (736×416 at 16:9). FastVideo returns the latents instead of decoding them
   (`output_type = "latent"`).
2. **Latent upscale**: our 3D convolutional upscaler (`studio/pipelines/h3_upscaler.py`) loads
   LBH-123-AI's checkpoint (Apache-2.0 weights) and enlarges the video latents to ≈0.9 MP with
   the reference node's "megapixels" sizing at alignment 32 (1280×736, factor 1.756). Like its
   ComfyUI node, it takes the DiT's latents normalised a second time with the VAE statistics,
   and its output is denormalised once. Fed the DiT's latents directly, it rendered pink mush on
   the GB10 (correlation with a trilinear upscale −0.01, against 0.86 with the second
   normalisation). Clips longer than 32 latent frames (5 s is 37) are processed in chunks of 32 with a
   5-frame cross-fade, as the reference node did with chunking on.
3. **Pass 2**: the workflow's refine pass is ComfyUI's "beta" scheduler, 3 steps, denoise 0.4 at
   shift 12: sigmas 0.890, 0.770, 0.482, 0. They fall on PDD nodes 19, 25, 30 and 32, which
   become the PDD plan of this request (3 transformer forwards). The upscaled video latents and
   the first pass's audio latents are noised to node 19 (x = (1 − σ)·latent + σ·noise, at the
   video and the audio sigma of that node) and injected as the starting latents. FastVideo
   then decodes the MP4.

Both passes use the same generator, seed, prompt and references.

## Precision and fallback

`STUDIO_H3_PRECISION` = `auto` (default), `nvfp4` or `fp8`.

- **NVFP4** follows FastVideo's DGX Spark recipes: packed NVFP4 transformer (`h3_dit_vsa`),
  FP4 sparse attention with an FP8 prefix, cuDNN FP4 GEMMs, and for OmniRef the 36-layer NVFP4
  video decoder and the bit-identical speed-ups. Everything stays resident in the GB10's
  unified memory.
- **FP8** uses the FP8 checkpoints, FP8 linears and bf16 sparse attention, as FastVideo's recipe
  for GPUs without FP4, still fully resident.
- **auto** starts on NVFP4. If the first NVFP4 job fails (kernels or packed weights not working
  on this GPU), the job downloads the FP8 checkpoint if needed, retries once on FP8, says so in
  its progress message, and H3 stays on FP8 until the server restarts. Once an NVFP4 job has
  succeeded, a later failure is reported, not retried. A bad input (a LoRA, an image) never
  triggers the fallback.

Left out on purpose: the precomputed AdaLN table of the recipes (it would bypass a LoRA on the
AdaLN layers and holds only the timesteps of the trained plan, not those of the refine pass)
and any speed-up LoRA (the checkpoints are already distilled).

## LoRA stack

FastVideo holds one adapter per layer of a packed transformer, so `studio/pipelines/h3_lora.py`
folds a stack into one adapter: each LoRA is converted to FastVideo's layout by FastVideo's own
community-LoRA converter, its strength and alpha/rank are folded into its B factor, and the
factors are concatenated along the rank. The product of the merged factors is exactly the sum of
the individual products. The merged file is cached under `STUDIO_DATA_DIR/cache/h3-loras/` and
loaded at strength 1 as a side branch next to the quantized weights
(`FASTVIDEO_LORA_SIDE_BRANCH=1`), never merged into them (LESSONS #30). A LoRA with full-weight
patches, or with keys that match no H3 layer, is refused rather than partly applied. Changing the
stack builds a new generator.

## Memory and cancellation

Engines stay resident while memory allows, as in ComfyUI: before one is loaded, the least
recently used are unloaded until its weights (the size of its model folder) plus 16 GB fit in
available memory. Krea 2 and Qwen Image 2.1 stay loaded together next to Gemma (about 80 GB on
the GB10). An H3 engine is loaded alone: its peak goes far past its weights (with Qwen kept
next to it, a two-pass r2v left 11 GB free). Switching between the V2 and the OmniRef
checkpoint, or between precisions or LoRA stacks, builds another FastVideo generator. FastVideo runs in-process (one GPU), so cancelling a
job interrupts the running pass at its next denoising step.

Krea 2 and Qwen Image 2.1 read their safetensors whole (`disable_mmap`, `common.LOAD_OPTIONS`):
memory-mapped, Krea 2 took 254 s to load on the GB10 although its disk reads 4.3 GB/s; read
whole, 86 s; loaded straight onto the GPU (`device_map`), 250 s (`tools/qualify/load_speed.sh`,
page cache dropped before each, 2026-11-01). Each load writes an `engine:` line to the
container log.

Measured on the GB10 (2026-10-30, NVFP4, text to video, 1280×736, 5 s, engine already resident):
190 s per clip, of which 149 s of denoising (8 steps × 18.6 s), 40 s of VAE decoding (not
compiled, see LESSONS #37) and under 1 s of muxing. The first job of a process adds about 55 s
of warm-up. Loading the engine from cold takes about 4 min 45: 170 s to read the 27 GB of bf16
shards to CPU, then 75 s of NVFP4 export. The first NVFP4 job on a machine also compiles a
FlashInfer kernel once, in about 3 min (LESSONS #36). Available memory never fell below 37 GB
during a render.

**Warm up before a demo.** The first render after each container (re)creation compiles kernels
(Triton, FlashInfer) and takes about 440 s for reference to video, against 208 s once warm. Run
one throwaway render before showing the studio.

Image references are sized as in the validated ComfyUI workflow (`ref_image_size` "max"): scaled
down to a 2048 px short edge when larger, never up, in multiples of 32. FastVideo's own rule
scales every image reference to a 2048 px short edge, upscaling the 1920×1088 sheets to
3616×2048, and its VSA-H3 attention keeps image references dense (only video references are
sparse). Reference to video then took 680 s with 2 references on the GB10, against 260 s in
ComfyUI. `h3_runtime` installs ComfyUI's rule in place of FastVideo's
`resolve_reference_image_size` when it builds the OmniRef engine: r2v with 2 references takes
208 s once warm, with no visible loss (LESSONS #38).

## What the front end does with it (step 4)

The prompt grammar stays in the front end (ADR 0001), ported from 1.4's `js/engine.js` in step 4:

| 1.4 feature | 2.0 job |
|---|---|
| Storyboard cut (keyframe → clip) | `h3-i2v`, `image` = the shot image, prompt from `buildH3CutPrompt` (alignment sentence with the rendered duration, then the 6 fields; every subject defined on `<Picture 1>`) |
| Relay segment, continuous link | `h3-i2v`, `image` = the last-frame PNG of the previous segment (output 1 of its job), used as is (LESSONS #32) |
| Relay segment with an end image | `h3-i2v` with `last_image` (FL2VA), alignment sentence with two marks |
| Reference2Video | `h3-r2v`, `references` = subject sheet, setting sheet, then extra references; prompt from `buildH3RefPrompt` |
| Text to video | `h3-t2v` |

LTX 2.5 is gone: no LTX engine, catalog entry or cut engine choice. Every cut is an H3 cut.

## Torch version

FastVideo pins `torch==2.12.0`; NGC PyTorch 25.11, the base image, ships 2.10 (`2.10.0a0+nv25.11`,
CUDA 13.0). The image keeps the NGC torch:

- FastVideo and fastvideo-kernel are installed without their dependencies, and the kernels are
  compiled against the NGC torch, so a C++ API change fails the build instead of the render.
- Every later `pip install` is constrained to the base image's torch, torchvision and triton
  (`/etc/studio-base-constraints.txt`), so no requirement can swap them for a generic wheel.
- Checked without a GPU on torch 2.10: the 390 `torch.*` names FastVideo uses all exist, and the
  53 FastVideo modules the H3 engines load all import. That check found two gaps in the first
  requirement list, now fixed: `ftfy`, `pyarrow`, `datasets` and `torchdata` are imported by
  `import fastvideo` itself, and FastVideo's `tokenizers<0.23` cannot be installed with
  transformers ≥ 5.16 (H3 uses the Qwen3-VL tokenizer, which the cap does not concern).

On the GB10 (2026-10-30), the Triton and CUDA kernels (VSA, NVFP4 GEMM through flashinfer and
cuDNN) ran on the NGC versions for every qualification case. If a later version breaks them, the
fallback is FastVideo's own GB10 stack: CUDA 13.0 with the torch 2.12 cu130 wheels (FastVideo
`docker/Dockerfile`, `TORCH_CUDA_ARCH_LIST=12.1a`). It needs the same driver as NGC 25.11, but the
image engines would then also run on the generic torch instead of the NGC build. NGC 26.04 and
26.05 ship torch 2.12 pre-releases, on CUDA 13.2, which needs a newer driver.

## Qualification

Qualified on the GB10 on 2026-10-30, after the renders below were inspected and accepted by the
project owner: `fasth3-v2-nvfp4`, `fasth3-omniref-nvfp4` and `h3-latent-upscaler`. The FP8
fallbacks (`fasth3-v2-fp8`, `fasth3-omniref-fp8`) were never rendered and stay
`"qualified": false` (LESSONS #1–#3). `tools/qualify/video_cases.json` holds
the cases, built from the 1.4 prompt grammar by `export_1_4_video_prompts.js`, with the subject
and sheets of the image cases:

```bash
python tools/qualify/run_image_cases.py --cases image_cases.json video_cases.json --report qualify.json
```

For each clip, look at the first, middle and last frames, the edges and sky gradients, and
listen to the audio. Specific checks:

- `r2v`: compare with the ComfyUI two-pass render (same prompt, sheets, seed): identity, time
  (≈260 s in ComfyUI), no colour shift after the refine pass, audio present.
- `r2v-preview`: the first pass alone, for speed.
- `cut-i2v`, `relay-2`: the clip starts on the image; segment 2 continues segment 1 without a
  visible cut.
- `cut-fl2va`: the clip goes from the shot image to the last frame of `cut-i2v` and lands on it;
  check a frame on each side of the middle.
- `t2v`: the scene matches the prompt.

First qualification run on the GB10 (2026-10-30, NVFP4, no FP8 fallback needed): `t2v`,
`cut-i2v`, `relay-2` and `r2v-preview` pass; `r2v` passes once the upscaler gets its second
normalisation. FastVideo's kernels run on the NGC 25.11 torch, and available memory never fell
below 26.8 GB. Known limits:

- **FL2VA ghosting.** `cut-fl2va` lands on its last image, but a second, ghost head appears from
  about frame 30 to frame 100. It shows when the first and last images differ strongly in
  framing. Unverified lead: the 1.4 cut prompt (`buildH3CutPrompt`) always writes the shot's
  camera motion, "steady framing with subtle natural movement" for a medium shot, which fights an
  end image that asks the framing to change. The step 4 port could leave the camera motion out
  when an end image is given; to check with a render.
- Face identity in `r2v` could not be measured: the character is seen from the back or in profile.
